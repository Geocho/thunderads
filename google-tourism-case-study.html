Grow Greek Tourism Online.
Teaching a country to be found.

The Brief

A national program is only as big as its turnout.

Grow Greek Tourism Online is Google's initiative to get Greek tourism businesses online, run with the Ministry of Tourism, the Greek National Tourism Organization and SETE. Free training, a national network of Google-trained advisors travelling to 30 destinations across 13 regions, and a free online learning platform. A genuinely good thing, offered for nothing.

The brief Thunderads took on: the campaign film. The program could only train the people who showed up — and the film's one job was to make tens of thousands of busy tourism professionals decide to show up.

The Problem

The world was searching for Greece.
Greek tourism wasn't there to be found.

Searches for "travel to Greece" were climbing fast, up 19% in a year, and up 72% on mobile. The demand was real and it was online. But only 8% of Greek hotels could take a booking through their own website. An entire industry was missing the customers actively looking for it. The training existed to close that gap. The film existed to get the industry into the room.

The Approach

Make digital skills feel like opportunity, not homework.

"Free seminar on digital marketing" sounds like an afternoon lost. To a hotelier mid-season, or a guide, or a family taverna, it competes with running the business. The film had to reframe the offer entirely: this isn't a class, it's the customers you're currently missing.

01
Recruit, don't lecture
The film had a single measurable job: enrolment. Every creative choice was judged against one question, does this make a tourism professional sign up. Not awareness. Sign-ups.
02
Sell the outcome, not the curriculum
Nobody wakes up wanting an analytics seminar. They want a fuller season, direct bookings, visitors all year round. The film led with the result the training unlocks, and let the curriculum stay in the background.
03
Make it feel national, not Athens-centric
Thirty destinations across thirteen regions. A hotelier on a small island and a tour operator in the north both had to watch the film and see their own business in it, not a campaign made for the capital.
04
Let peers carry the proof
The most persuasive argument wasn't Google's. It was other Greek tourism businesses already winning with the tools, the operators who had doubled turnover or multiplied their bookings. Real results from real peers beat any promise.
05
Production worthy of the logos
One film had to carry Google, the Ministry of Tourism, GNTO and SETE together. It had to look like a national initiative the whole industry could trust, and be produced to that bar.

The Result

The room filled up.
And kept filling.

The film fronted one of the largest digital-skills efforts ever run for a single industry in Greece. Twenty Google-trained advisors on the road across thirty destinations and thirteen regions, a free national learning platform behind them. Across its run, Grow Greek Tourism Online went on to train more than 140,000 Greek tourism professionals and small businesses, and the businesses that took it up have the numbers to show for it.
