The Brief

A year of work. One film to carry it.

Every year, Mastercard's Area Marketing team for Greece, Cyprus and Malta looks back at twelve months of campaigns, sponsorships, product launches, and social-impact work, all of it real, all of it earned. The brief was a 2024 year-in-review film, around two and a half minutes, built to open a townhall.

It didn't stay local. The film cleared the bar at regional level and was approved to present up the chain. The hard part was never the content. It was making people actually want to watch it.

The Problem

The year-in-review is where
attention goes to die.

Most corporate recaps are a slideshow of achievements set to stock music. They scroll numbers nobody reads, over footage nobody remembers, in a tone nobody believes. By minute one, the room is on its phone. For a brand whose whole idea is Priceless, a forgettable recap of a remarkable year was the one outcome we couldn't accept.

The Approach

Direct, data-driven, never dull.

We made the promise part of the brief and then built every decision around it. The film had to carry a full year of business results and still feel like something you'd choose to watch.

01
A pop-culture spine
2024 had its own language: the rollercoaster of trends, "very demure, very mindful", robots doing backflips, the eternal pineapple-pizza argument. We opened in that world, so the audience was already leaning in before the first Mastercard number landed.
02
Tone that shifts on purpose
Bold where the brand dominated. Funny where the data could breathe. Clean where the numbers had to be precise. Emotional where the impact was real. Five registers, one film, no whiplash.
03
Short lines. Fast cuts. No drag.
Every line was written to be heard once and land. Punchy sentences, quick supers, a rhythm engineered so the eye never has time to wander. Two and a half minutes that move like ninety seconds.
04
Jokes that carry the data
"We lapped giants like Amazon, Apple and Microsoft." "Loud enough to be heard from Mars." "Champagne is not included, but we're working on it." The humour isn't decoration. It's the delivery system that gets a hard metric remembered.
05
References, not guesses
The look, the supers and the tone of voice were each anchored to a clear reference. We weren't improvising a style. We were hitting a target everyone could see before a frame was cut.

The Result

It cleared the local bar.
Then the regional one.

The film delivered a full year of campaigns, sponsorships, product launches and social-impact work in one sitting, fast enough that no one reached for a phone. It didn't stay a Greece, Cyprus and Malta piece: it was approved by Beatrice Cornacchia, Senior Vice President of Marketing and Communications for Mastercard's EEMEA region, to present up the chain. Direct, data-driven, never dull. As promised.
