The Brief

A new startup. The first time on radio. Easter, of all weeks.

Easter is the largest annual Greek migration. Ports rammed. Roads gridlocked. Helicopters? Most travellers still don't think of them as an option. Hoper, a young aviation startup, had to introduce itself to the country during the week the country can't sit still, and on a medium the brand had never used.

No big budget. No legacy brand equity. Everyone else in the category was playing safe. We went the other way.

The Problem

The sacred week makes everyone
play it safe.

Easter in Greece is wall-to-wall lambs, candles, and earnest travel-safety copy. The category sounds the same in every spot. For a first-time radio advertiser with a 28-second slot and a small media plan, blending in was the most expensive thing we could have done.

The Approach

Take the most sacred sound of Easter and turn it into a sales tool.

We wrote a radio spot in the form of an Orthodox tropario, a liturgical chant. Two cult Greek voices delivered it straight-faced. The refrain, repeated four times per spot in cathedral cadence, was the tagline:

By helicopter!

01
Test the medium
Hoper had never run radio. We treated the campaign as a real-world test of whether radio can move both awareness and ticket sales for a new aviation brand. The KPIs were calibrated for both.
02
Go loud. Go weird.
With no media budget to brute-force frequency, the creative had to do the lifting. The brief to ourselves: write something nobody can scroll past, even at 6:45am on the way to a port.
03
Borrow a sacred frame
Easter is the deepest cultural moment in Greece. The Orthodox tropario is its sound. We didn't parody it, we used it, faithfully, as the structural form of the spot. The collision between liturgical cadence and a helicopter sales pitch did all the work.
04
Build a catchphrase, not a slogan
Slogans get written down. Catchphrases get repeated by hosts the next morning. We engineered By helicopter! to land four times in 28 seconds, in the right cadence, so it left the spot and entered the broadcast.
05
Cast cult voices, not narrator voices
Spyros Bibilas, arguably the most recognisable cult voice in Greece, paired with Christodoulos Stylianou, whose liturgical authority carries the sacred frame credibly. The casting alone earned the spot a second listen.

Beyond Easter

One cultural moment was the test. The format was the point.

The Easter campaign proved the approach. Black Friday proved it travels. Hoper kept the same play, take the loudest moment on the calendar and own its sound, and pointed it at a different season. The catchphrase logic, the cut-through, the made-for-radio writing: a system, not a one-off.

The Result

The catchphrase outran the spot.
The sales followed.

Inside the campaign window, Hoper ticket sales rose +32%. Radio hosts started repeating By helicopter! on their own broadcasts. Listeners phoned the Hoper call centre to ask about "the spot with the priests". The first time on radio for the brand became the year's most-quoted radio sound, and walked off with the Gold Grand at the Ermis Awards 2025.
